Two taps and a swipe

Each piece falls toward its slot, drawn as a ghost on the solid. Tap the right or left half of the screen to rotate it a quarter turn; when it lines up, swipe up and it shoots into place. A misaligned piece bounces and stays in the air while another one comes in; with eight in the air, the sky is full and the game is over. Every second you save earns points, and rescues and master pieces help when things get tight. No reading required: the guided first round, with no timer and no way to lose, teaches you the controls.

28 polyhedra, 234 stellations

Platonic, Archimedean and Catalan solids, prisms, antiprisms, bipyramids and trapezohedra. Nothing is modeled by hand: the game computes every solid on your device from its geometry. Seven cards explain what a polyhedron, a stellation and a cell are.

The 59 icosahedra

In 1938, Coxeter, Du Val, Flather and Petrie counted the fifty-nine stellations of the icosahedron, and Flather built them in cardboard, one by one. In Stellaria they are the final prize of the full game: earned by completing everything else, then assembled one by one.

From your screen to your desk

Every stellation you complete is kept in the Gallery, where you can turn it, zoom in and export it as an STL file. With a 3D printer, the star you just built can end up on your desk.

Free to start, pay once

Free with no time limit: four polyhedra with their ten stellations, the guided first round, the Gallery and STL export. “The full game” unlocks the other 24 polyhedra with a single purchase. No subscriptions, no ads, no accounts, no data collection. Works offline. Game Center is optional: achievements and leaderboards if you want them; if not, your records stay on your device.
